# Cartografia Quiet

Cartografia Quiet treats public data as a field specimen: precise, imperfect, and worth observing without theatrics. Space carries the argument. Thin lines, measured gaps and restrained typography make the work feel like a carefully assembled research plate, the product of deep expertise rather than decoration. Every slide should look meticulously crafted, with the kind of spacing and alignment that suggests someone labored over the page long after it was already "good enough".

The visual language is built from maps without drawing literal maps. Administrative borders become hairline grids, data joins become narrow paths, and uncertainty appears as quiet interruptions in otherwise orderly systems. Color is sparse. A dark ink base, a pale paper field and one teal signal give the composition discipline. Purple is allowed only as a secondary analytical trace, never as a generic technology glow.

Scale does most of the storytelling. One number can fill a slide; a small label can sit beside it like a lab note. The viewer should feel the weight of a national dataset before reading a sentence about it. Text stays essential: short claims, real numbers, no paragraphs. The design should reward a second look through painstaking attention to micro-rhythm, repeated marks and master-level execution.

The composition should carry tension between public-service seriousness and portfolio confidence. It is not a government report, but it respects the subject. It is not a marketing deck, but it knows it has to stop someone scrolling. The result should feel like a social artifact made by a data scientist with taste: clear enough for LinkedIn, rigorous enough for GitHub, and polished enough to stand on its own as visual work.

The quiet conceptual reference is "cadastro nao e acesso". It should not be shouted. It should be embedded as a recurring visual split: record versus evidence, point versus territory, count versus operation. Each slide shows one layer becoming less naive. The final work should feel painstakingly assembled from evidence, not generated from a template.
